## Problem Statement

Tallyhand should let an independent contractor or their agent carry a job from commercial agreement through billing, verified collection and delivery in one workspace. Absorb Agora’s full existing sales/payment feature set and retire its separate runtime after verified migration.

Start with a real monthly billing job: turn a messy timesheet into an accurate reviewed invoice, deliver it with authorization, and follow collection with honest payment/payout evidence. Existing manual workflows and useful access through the user’s own agent remain free. Test an optional hosted billing service later, using the same domain and interfaces.

- They tested the agent flow and called it “magical,” but clarified “Haven’t tried” when asked why they did not use it for real billing. No observed trust or onboarding failure is established.
- A coworker will start using Tallyhand. Their current workflow and actual outcome are unknown.
- The founder reports autonomous AgentID signups and records created for two users; invoice use, repeat use, real collections, payer identity and willingness to pay are unknown.

These are evidence of personal invoice value and early external activation. They are not evidence of paying software customers or a validated hosted-service market.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

First pilot: the founder and their coworker, working on actual monthly contracting invoices. Their own existing agents can prepare drafts through Tallyhand’s surfaces; no new hosted agent is required to learn from the pilot.

Paid hypothesis: optional monthly billing assistance, including operation of a bounded routine, delivery/retry handling, collection status and exceptions. Its value must be demonstrated in a real workflow before choosing a price. A successful demo or record creation alone does not qualify.

## Accepted Decisions and Constraints

- Preserve the useful free core and access for users’ own agents. Hosted execution is an optional service, not a prerequisite for using the domain.
- Focus the first release on monthly billing; retain complete Agora absorption and retirement as the destination.
- Use Tallyhand’s identity, client records, application, storage contracts, CLI, REST and MCP. Port Agora’s rules; adapt its runtime, tenant and currency assumptions.
- Keep Codex/other-agent instructions canonical in AGENTS.md; CLAUDE.md references it. Model/provider choice is not a product dependency.
- AgentID signup creates an agent account/workspace. It does not grant access to another human’s workspace, payment account or customer communications.
- Online financial outcomes are server-authoritative. Local/browser-only users retain manual billing and drafts, with online capability limits reported honestly.
- Invoice delivery, reminders, refunds and external-account connections require explicit authority. Preparing a draft does not imply permission to send it.
- Defer marketplaces, shipping, inventory, escrow, autonomous payouts and new subscription billing until demand supports them.

## Recommended Approach

Create first-party sales/payments domain services in Tallyhand. Keep provider-specific network/credentials code in adapters, and financial invariants in the domain. Plugins and outgoing integrations react to committed events rather than independently deciding balances.

One Client links commercial terms, an Order when applicable, optional Projects, Invoices, Payments, Refunds and Deliverables. An order describes the agreement; an invoice is a billing claim; a payment is money; a project organizes work. Do not count one sale twice across these records.

### Free product and optional paid service

The free product remains usable through the UI and existing agent surfaces: work records, invoicing, exports, core sales/payment records and authorized provider operations. External payment-provider fees and supported-backend limits remain explicit.

The potential paid service operates a routine: schedule work, keep durable progress, retry supported failures, surface exceptions, and report outcomes. Use the same tools and financial rules as external agents. Start by measuring an external-agent pilot; add a hosted runner only when repeated use demonstrates what it needs to do.

Price, included usage, support promises, model costs and bank-connection costs remain open. Avoid unlimited-cost promises. Compare workspace/service pricing with a bounded usage allowance after measuring actual cost; no per-agent seat fee or percentage of contracting revenue is selected.

## Product Experience

- Clients show accepted estimates/orders alongside project, invoice and payment history.
- Billing contains invoices, estimates, payments and a Services & Products catalog. An order can appear as an accepted agreement or sale detail.
- Invoice timelines distinguish draft, delivery, customer payment, refund, provider-reported payout and bank-confirmed receipt.
- Projects gain Deliverables with claim/complete/fail/retry and explicit payment prerequisites. Existing Task records remain time entries.
- Customer links support revision-specific estimate review/acceptance, checkout, payment status and receipts. A link to one document does not implicitly grant access to the entire client history.
- Connections & Automation exposes supported payment backends, agent scopes/limits, approval queues, signed webhook delivery/replay and diagnostics.

Hourly, fixed-price and quick-sale paths share this model. A project remains optional for a simple product/service sale. Deposits and milestone schedules are proposed extensions; Agora currently supports a single full payment per order.

### Acceptance and refund behavior

A merchant publishes an estimate as an offer ready for acceptance. A valid customer acceptance of its current unexpired revision immediately creates the accepted order and collection request; no second merchant approval is required. The customer sees the accepted terms and payment progress. A separately authorized merchant API acceptance can also create an order, but is labeled merchant-initiated and does not assert customer acceptance. The first successful acceptance fixes the revision and order; repeated or competing attempts return that existing outcome or an explicit stale-revision/unavailable result without creating another sale. Subsequent customer consent, if collected, is separate evidence rather than a rewritten acceptance history.

For Agora's initial one-payment sale, a confirmed full refund cancels every unfinished deliverable, including awaiting-payment, ready, claimed and failed work. Cancelled work cannot be claimed, completed or retried. Partial refunds do not automatically cancel delivery. A pending refund is shown as pending and does not yet assert cancellation. If completion commits before refund confirmation, keep its completion evidence and display it alongside the refund; if refund confirmation commits first, reject later completion/retry. External work that cannot be stopped is surfaced for owner review rather than hidden or deleted.

When multiple payments are supported, the equivalent full-refund condition is confirmed refunds reducing an order's cumulative confirmed payment balance to zero after it previously received payment. Refund of one payment while other net payments remain is a partial order refund and does not automatically cancel its deliverables. Additional milestone-specific hold/cancellation behavior is outside the first release and must be designed explicitly before that extension ships.

## Financial and Automation Foundation

- Add PaymentRequest, PaymentAttempt, Payment, Allocation, Refund, Approval, ProviderEvent, AuditEvent and WebhookDelivery records. Add catalog/estimate revisions, acceptance, orders and deliverables when their release slice begins.
- Store validated integer minor units and currency metadata for financial records. Freeze invoice/accepted-offer terms and apply explicit conversion at legacy decimal boundaries; publish supported online currencies.
- Persist attempts before network calls, use stable provider idempotency keys, and reconcile unknown responses. Application receipt caching alone cannot prevent duplicate external work.
- Verify provider signatures and deduplicate event IDs. Validate workspace, account, mode, amount, currency and stored attempt before allocating money.
- Use atomic domain methods for allocation, refund reservations, quote acceptance, deliverable claims and event enqueueing. Implement database transactions/Convex mutations; asynchronous CRUD sequences are insufficient.
- Reserve pending refund allowances; approvals do not bypass revoked grants, workspace boundaries or provider verification. Record who requested, approved and executed each action.
- Separate financial permissions from broad workspace write scopes. Human owners and agents use explicit policies for payment/refund/delivery, test/live mode and amount limits.
- Preserve manual payment attribution and evidence. Do not convert a manual paid flag into a fabricated provider event.
- Make background jobs resumable with durable states, retries and visible failures. Outgoing webhook delivery is independent of the transaction that commits the business change.

The initial source audit found Convex Stripe Connect unsupported and narrow checkout-completed handling. Revalidate those findings before implementation, then complete the backend actually used by hosted customers; keep SQLite financial support for self-hosting. Other backends must either meet atomic guarantees or report unsupported online operations.

### What “paid” means

A successful customer payment, Stripe-reported payout and independently confirmed bank receipt are different facts. Track them separately.

For Stripe-supported collection, expose payment and payout progress and reconcile automatic payout components. Bank-confirmed receipt requires a supported bank feed, imported statement or explicit user confirmation. Direct external ACH/check payments use their own evidence/manual path. No generic bank coverage or automatic settlement guarantee is promised.

## 1. Architecture review

### A1 — Durable collection intent and atomic money recording

[P1] (confidence: 9/10) `src/plugins/stripe/stripe-client.ts:77–85` — the checkout POST includes ``authorization: `Bearer ${secretKey}`,``, `"content-type": "application/x-www-form-urlencoded",` and `...(params.stripeAccount ? { "stripe-account": params.stripeAccount } : {}),` but no `Idempotency-Key`. `src/app/api/v1/stripe/webhook/route.ts:87–90` separately runs `await provider.markInvoicePaid(invoiceId);`, `await provider.getInvoice(invoiceId);`, then `await pluginRegistry.emit("onInvoicePaid", updated);`. A retryable network request and a paid flag are insufficient to implement the plan's durable attempts, one allocation and committed events (`design:88–90,116`). This is a current interface gap, not a demonstrated production duplicate charge.

Accepted contract: before external work, atomically create/reuse a PaymentRequest and PaymentAttempt with immutable owner, account, mode, invoice revision, amount, currency and canonical request fingerprint. The operation identity survives API response-cache expiry. A changed body under the same operation identity conflicts. Concurrent clicks/agents reuse the same outstanding collection intent rather than relying on a client-supplied receipt key alone. Check current authority when starting/retrying external work. Provider idempotency is tied to the stored attempt; an uncertain response moves to reconciliation, not a fresh logical charge. A reconciler and webhook processor call the same atomic outcome method. Uniqueness is enforced for scoped provider payment identity as well as event identity: different event IDs referring to one payment still produce one Payment/Allocation. In one commit, record the financial outcome, append audit history and enqueue the committed notification. Notification retries do not change balances.

Existing full-invoice checkout remains the initial collection path. Deposits, milestone collection and an optional managed runner retain their later design timing. No external exactly-once-delivery promise is made: provider/outgoing delivery may repeat; domain allocation is deduplicated.

### A3 — Verified event/refund reconciliation, independent of mutable invoice flags

[P1] (confidence: 9/10) `src/app/api/v1/stripe/webhook/route.ts:50` — `if (event.type === "checkout.session.completed")`; lines 75/81 compare `Math.round(invoice.total * 100)` with `session.amount_total >= expectedCents`; the event shape at lines 39–43 includes type/account/data, not a persisted event ID or mode. Required refund/delayed-outcome history is not implemented by this checkout-only handler (`design:89,100–102,114–116`).

Accepted contract: verify the raw-body signature, durably ingest the scoped provider event, and acknowledge only after that receipt is committed. Persist processing state so a crash after acknowledgement is recoverable. Reject/quarantine mismatched owner/account/mode/currency/amount/attempt identity with a visible reason. Resolve authoritative provider payment/refund objects when event order or payload completeness requires it; a refund may arrive before its payment event. Pending/failed refunds do not subtract confirmed money; confirmed refunds subtract once. Reconciliation and event processing converge on the same financial record even when Stripe retries or emits several related events. Do not infer payment from a redirect query parameter.

Legacy paid flags migrate with manual/unknown provenance, never fabricated Stripe evidence. Track gross confirmed payments, confirmed refunds and net allocated collection separately from the invoice's historical paid state. Checkout success, provider payout and independently confirmed bank receipt remain separate timeline facts.
